# CANChat Agent — Pilot Survey

Thank you for taking part in the CANChat Agent pilot. This survey covers three things we care about
most: **usability** (is it easy and pleasant to use?), **usefulness** (does it help you get real work
done?), and **performance** (is it fast and reliable enough?). It also covers trust, safety, and
privacy, because an agent that acts inside your browser has to earn your confidence.

There are no wrong answers. Critical feedback is the most valuable feedback. Please answer based on
what actually happened, not on what you think we hope to hear.

> **Time required:** about 40–50 minutes for the full survey. The **[Core]** parts (A–I, N–R) take about
> 30 minutes in total; parts tagged **[If used]** (J–M) only apply to features you touched. Skip
> anything you did not use: tick "Did not use" and move on.

---

## How to use this document

- **Participants:** fill this in over the pilot period, not all at once. Part D (weekly check-in) is
  meant to be repeated. Parts E–O are best completed after you have used the relevant feature.
- **Scales used throughout** (unless a question says otherwise):

  | Scale | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | **Agreement** | Strongly disagree | Disagree | Neutral | Agree | Strongly agree |
  | **Frequency** | Never | Rarely | Sometimes | Often | Always |
  | **Quality** | Very poor | Poor | Acceptable | Good | Excellent |
  | **Ease** | Very difficult | Difficult | Neither | Easy | Very easy |

  Write your answer as a number, or tick the box. **N/A** always means "did not use / no opinion."
- **Free-text answers:** short is fine. Specific examples ("it took me three tries to find the
  Settings page") are worth more than general statements ("navigation is bad").
- **Screenshots** of anything confusing, wrong, or broken are very welcome. Please **redact
  sensitive or Protected information** before sharing them.

> **Do not include Protected, personal, or sensitive content** from your real work in your survey
> answers. Describe the *kind* of task ("summarizing a policy document from SharePoint") rather than
> pasting its contents.

# Appendix 2 — Analysis guide (for the pilot lead)

### Scoring

| Measure | How to compute | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **SUS** (Part C) | Odd items: response − 1. Even items: 5 − response. Sum × 2.5 | < 51 poor · 51–68 below average · 68–80 good · > 80 excellent |
| **NPS** (B5) | % promoters (9–10) − % detractors (0–6) | > 0 acceptable · > 30 good · > 50 excellent |
| **Sean Ellis test** (B4) | % "Very disappointed" | ≥ 40% suggests product-market fit for the segment |
| **Task success** (Appendix 1) | Successes ÷ attempts | Below 78% = usability problem to prioritize |
| **SEQ** | Mean of 1–7 ratings per task | < 5.5 flags a difficult task |
| **Adoption** (D1, D9) | Days/week trend, drop-off week | Sharp week-2 drop signals novelty-only value |
| **Perceived speed** (H1, H4) | Mean; % rating ≥ 4 | Flag rows < 3.5 |
| **Measured speed** (H.5) | Median and p90 per scenario, across participants | Compare with H2 tolerance thresholds |
| **Accuracy** (G7, G8, G13) | % of participants reporting wrong/unsupported answers or wrong citations | Any "significant consequence" (G10) is a priority finding |
| **Approval load** (I2, I3) | Distribution; correlation with usage | "Far too many" plus "approved without reading" = prompt fatigue risk |

### Suggested cuts of the data

- By **experience**: A5 and A6 (AI-savvy vs. new users) — do novices struggle with setup and concepts?
- By **role** (A2) — which roles get the most value?
- By **language** (A9, Part N) — does French use hit more friction?
- By **model/endpoint** (A10) — are quality and speed complaints really model-specific?
- By **usage intensity** (A12, D1) — do heavy users rate differently from light users?
- By **feature usage** (A13) — who used what, and how does feature usage correlate with satisfaction?

### Priority framework

Score each issue **Impact** (1–5: how badly it hurts users) × **Frequency** (1–5: how many users
hit it), then sort. Treat these as release-blocking regardless of score:

1. Any **security or privacy** issue (P3), or any action taken without approval.
2. Any **data loss** (P2, F13).
3. Any answer that caused a significant consequence when acted on (G10).
4. Any accessibility barrier that blocks a task (N3).
5. First-run failures that prevent a participant from getting an answer (E3, E6).

### Reporting

Deliver a short readout covering: (1) headline SUS/NPS/adoption; (2) the top five findings across
usability, usefulness, and performance; (3) the measured-speed table; (4) a ranked fix list (Q7, Q8 +
priority framework); (5) direct quotes from B6, B7, R2, R3; and (6) a go / no-go / go-with-conditions
recommendation for wider rollout.
